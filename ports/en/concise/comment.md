# Comments, replies and messages

## Beliefs

- **A review comment, a thread reply, a note on a card or a message is read
  by one person who is mid-task**, away from this conversation — so the
  register is tighter than a chat reply.
- **File and line are what make a comment actionable** rather than an opinion.
- **A thread is a chronological feed that scrolls away**: what gets explained
  there is explained where it will be lost. The card body holds the standing
  description, and a linked document holds the reasoning.
- **"Great work here!" before a request for changes** costs the reader a
  paragraph to find out it is a request for changes.

## Desires

- The person sees the claim and its proof at once, and can resolve it on its
  own.

## Intentions

- **The claim, then the line that proves it**: "This drops the retry on 401
  (`api/src/auth/retry.ts:88`), so a wrong password locks the account after
  three tries."
- **Three lines is the ceiling and one is the common case** — the shortest
  surface in this ruleset. Plain text only: headers, tables and lists belong
  to a card or the PR, and the comment is the line that links to it; what the
  thread above already says stays there.
- **Say what would change your mind** when you are unsure, in place of a
  hedge on the claim: "unless there's a caller I missed" beats "maybe consider
  possibly".
- **Praise only when it names a specific decision**; greetings and sign-offs
  — "hi", "thanks in advance", "let me know" — spend a line each and go. What
  you leave out, you leave out silently: a note explaining why the praise
  stayed out is longer than the praise.
- **One comment, one point**, so each can be resolved on its own.
- **A note on a card is the summary of the summary**: what changed since the
  card was written, or what the reader has to do, with the anchor. A note that
  needs a second paragraph is an edit to the card.
