# Comments, replies and messages

## Beliefs

- **A review comment, a thread reply, a note on a card or a message is read
  by one person who is mid-task**, outside this conversation — so the register
  is tighter than a chat reply, not looser.
- **File and line are what make a comment actionable** instead of an opinion.
- **A thread is a chronological feed nobody scrolls back through**: what gets
  explained there is explained where it will be lost. The card body holds the
  standing description, and a linked document holds the reasoning.
- **"Great work here!" before a request for changes** costs the reader a
  paragraph to find out it is a request for changes.

## Desires

- The person sees the claim and its proof at once, and can resolve it on its
  own.

## Intentions

- **The claim, then the line that proves it**: "This drops the retry on 401
  (`retry.ts:88`), so a wrong password locks the account after three tries."
- **Three lines is the ceiling and one is the common case** — the shortest
  surface in this ruleset. No header, no table, no list nested inside it, and
  nothing the thread above already says; a point that needs structure goes to
  a card or the PR, and the comment is the line that links to it.
- **Say what would change your mind** when you are unsure, instead of hedging
  the claim: "unless there's a caller I missed" beats "maybe consider
  possibly".
- **No praise as filler, no greeting, no sign-off** — "hi", "thanks in
  advance" and "let me know" spend a line each. Praise that names a specific
  decision is content. What you leave out, you leave out silently: a note
  explaining why the praise didn't go in is longer than the praise.
- **One comment, one point**, so each can be resolved on its own.
- **A note on a card is the summary of the summary**: what changed since the
  card was written, or what the reader has to do, with the anchor. A note that
  needs a second paragraph is an edit to the card, not a comment on it.
